# Universal Mail MCP — product description

## Headline

**Your AI is brilliant. It just can't find your email.**

Alternates:
- All your email. One conversation.
- The missing wire between your AI and your inbox.
- Every inbox you own. One assistant that finally knows about them.

## Short version (about 50 words)

Your AI can do almost anything — except find your email. Universal Mail MCP
connects all your accounts — Yahoo, Gmail, iCloud and more — to your AI through
a private server you own. One command to set up, designed to run free, and
careful by design: it reads and organizes, and sends only when you say so.

## Full version

Here's a peculiar fact about the moment we're living in. The assistant on your
screen can draft a eulogy, explain a mortgage, and untangle a spreadsheet
formula at two in the morning. But ask it to find the note your boat mechanic
sent in March, and it goes quiet.

That's not a problem of intelligence. It's a problem of plumbing.

Consider where your email actually lives. The Yahoo address you opened in 2004.
The Gmail from two jobs ago. The iCloud account your phone talked you into. A
work domain. A club address you check twice a year. Five mailboxes, five
logins, five walled gardens — and your AI standing outside every gate.

Universal Mail MCP opens the gates. Carefully.

It connects every email account you have — Yahoo, Gmail, iCloud, Fastmail, AOL,
Zoho and more — to your AI, through a private server that you own. It runs in
your own Google Cloud account. Not ours. Yours. There's no middleman service
holding your passwords.

Then you simply talk.

*"Find the Marriott timeshare email."*
*"Archive every LinkedIn job alert from this week."*
*"Draft a reply to Tom saying Thursday works."*

It searches every account at once and tells you where it found things. It does
the tedious part. You keep the judgment.

**Setup is the part people dread, so we designed it for people who have never
typed a command in their lives.** Click a button. Paste a password for each
account. Press Enter. About fifteen minutes later, you're done — and "done"
means tested, because it won't say so until every account has passed its
checks. If something goes wrong, it doesn't hand you an error code and a shrug.
It tells you, in plain English, exactly what to click next.

It's designed to run within Google Cloud's free tier. Google asks for a card on
file; Universal Mail sets a $1 alarm so nothing ever surprises you.

**And it's built around a simple idea about trust: freedom, with a few firm
edges.**

Your AI can read and organize freely. Sending mail as you stays off until you
turn it on — with your fingerprint or face. It never marks a message read just
by looking at it. It never files mail into a folder it guessed at. When it isn't
sure whether something happened, it tells you instead of trying again. And it
treats every email as information, never as instructions — because the next
clever scam won't arrive as a phone call. It'll arrive as an email your AI
reads.

You stay in charge from one simple page: every account, every connected app,
what each can do, and when it last did it. Disconnect anything with a single
click.

It works with Claude today, and it's built for ChatGPT and the other AI apps
adopting the same open standard.

The result isn't a flashier AI. It's a more useful one — the difference between
an assistant who knows everything and one who knows *your* things.

**Universal Mail MCP. All your email. Your AI. Your rules.**

## Before you publish (not copy)

This describes the product as designed. Nothing is built yet. Check each claim
against the real thing before it goes public:

- **"About fifteen minutes"** — confirmed only when Phase 3's exit test passes:
  a first-time user, one account, instructions only.
- **"Works with Claude today"** — true for v1 today; re-verify on the new
  version.
- **ChatGPT** — the copy says "built for", not "works with". Keep it that way
  until a real ChatGPT connection passes.
- **"Designed to run within Google Cloud's free tier"** — keep "designed to".
  Costs depend on use, and Google requires a card.
- **Provider list** — only the launch profiles. No Outlook or Microsoft 365 until
  that follow-up ships.
